Read this before you rank anything

  • These are today's category labels, back-applied. SEBI only forced scheme categorisation in 2017–18, and Flexi Cap did not exist as a category until November 2020. In 2010 most of these were simply "diversified equity" schemes, so a fund's 2010 mandate may not match the box it now sits in.
  • Regular plan, not Direct. Direct plans only launched on 1 January 2013. Every 2010 return here is therefore net of the full distributor-loaded expense ratio — roughly 0.6–1.2%/yr worse than the Direct plan you would buy today.
  • Survivorship bias, and it is large. Only schemes still reporting NAV today can be measured. Funds merged away or wound up between 2010 and now are invisible here, so every category median below is flattered. This is a survivors' league table, not the 2010 universe.
  • Point-to-point, one year. A single calendar year says almost nothing about skill. 2010 was a mild-up year that masked what came next in 2011.

Method. Scheme universe pulled from the AMFI-derived mfapi.in scheme list (37,743 schemes), narrowed to non-Direct Growth-option schemes with codes predating 2010, then filtered to equity, hybrid and solution-oriented SEBI categories. For each, the full NAV history was fetched and CY2010 return computed as NAV(last trading day ≤ 31-12-2010) / NAV(last trading day ≤ 31-12-2009) − 1. A scheme is included only if it already had a NAV on or before 15 Dec 2009, so no part-year return is counted as a full year — schemes were dropped on that test. A further scheme was dropped for a gap in its NAV feed at a year boundary (SBI Infrastructure's last 2009 NAV is dated 02-04-2009, which would have booked a 21-month move as a 2010 return). Index returns are price returns from Yahoo Finance (^NSEI, ^BSESN); fund NAVs are total-return by construction, which flatters funds against the index by roughly 1–1.5%/yr.

Educational analysis of public NAV data. Not investment advice, and 2010 performance carries no information about 2026.
