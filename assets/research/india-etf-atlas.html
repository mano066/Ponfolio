ETF research engine

Type what you want in plain words, or pick a starting point below. The engine finds every NSE ETF relevant to your request, including broad, factor and sector routes to similar exposure. It explains each underlying index, compares the ETFs on cost, liquidity, tracking, risk and returns, and ends with a decision framework. It never picks the biggest or best-performing ETF for you.

Index dictionary — what every name means

Every index behind an NSE ETF, plus common indices that have no ETF. Stocks is the number of companies (ranges or ≈ where the rules allow the count to vary; it changes at each rebalance). Summaries follow the NSE, BSE and MSCI methodology documents; for the exact current constituents, check the index factsheet.

Factors explained — momentum, quality, value and the rest

A factor index starts from a broad universe (such as Nifty 200 or Smallcap 250), scores every stock on one trait, and keeps only the top scorers. You still get that universe's size exposure, plus a deliberate tilt. Each panel lists every index built on the factor, best 5-year return first, with its measured risk. Nifty 50 sits at the bottom as the benchmark to beat. The button runs the research engine on that factor.

Why the early columns are short. A year only appears for an index when at least one ETF on it had already been trading for the whole year. Before 2016 only a handful of indices had an ETF (Nifty 50, Next 50, Bank, PSU Bank, Gold, Nasdaq 100, Hang Seng, Infra, Shariah and a few more), so those columns are genuinely short. Nothing is back-filled from index data.

Complete fund-house roster — every ETF on NSE

All ETFs from NSE's official list, grouped by index. Each row shows that fund's own calendar-year return from its own NAV, so houses on the same index differ slightly: that gap is what fees and tracking error cost you. Best 1Y marks the house that delivered the most over the last year, net of all costs. Most traded marks the highest average daily traded value on NSE over the last 60 sessions; an ETF nobody trades can be hard to sell near its NAV. An orange outline marks a year more than 6 points away from the peer median, usually a dividend payout or a scheme merger.

Tax treatment by ETF type

The rules as they stood for FY 2026-27 (carried over from the Finance Acts 2023–2025). Rules change with each Budget, so confirm with a tax adviser before acting. Holding period is counted from the date you bought the units.

Method. Each ETF's NSE listing is matched to its AMFI scheme by ISIN, and its full daily NAV history comes from AMFI (via mfapi.in). Unit splits (1:10, 1:100 and so on, which AMFI does not adjust) are detected and undone so returns stay continuous. The year-end value is the last NAV on or before 31 December; a calendar-year return needs NAVs at both year-ends, with the ETF listed before the first one. 2026 is year-to-date through and is not annualised. An index's yearly figure is the median across its fund houses. Trailing CAGRs run from the NAV nearest the start date to the latest NAV. Traded value is NSE close × volume averaged over 60 sessions (Yahoo Finance daily bars). Two new ETFs that AMFI has no ISIN match for yet (HDFCNIMEG, SILVER001) use NSE closing prices instead. Nippon's BeES ETFs changed AMFI scheme codes in 2011 and 2016; their older Benchmark and Goldman Sachs NAV records are joined on, which is why Nifty 50, Bank, Gold and PSU Bank reach back to 2010.

Limits. These are NAV (price) returns: dividends paid out as cash are not added back, so dividend-paying ETFs such as older Nifty 50 and Bank BeES units look up to ~1%/yr lower than their total return. Liquid ETFs that pay daily income as extra units keep their NAV flat near ₹1,000, so they show ~0% and are left out of the Liquid index figure. AMFI has no NAVs for ICICI Prudential's ETFs between late 2016 and mid-2018, so those funds (and Bharat 22, whose only ETF is ICICI's) have genuine blanks there. International ETFs can trade well above NAV when the overseas limit is shut. TER and AUM come from Groww's public ETF pages (a few from Tickertape), matched by ISIN and fetched 24 Sep 2026, because AMFI's own site was unreachable. TER is the current figure; AUM is month-end 31 Aug 2026 (GOLDBEES checked against Nippon's factsheet). They cover 337 and 345 of 351 ETFs. Nine TERs reported above SEBI's ETF cap were treated as errors and left blank. Tracking error is measured against the median of other ETFs on the same index (weekly, last 12 months), because index total-return series aren't published in a machine-readable form. Gold and silver ETFs show higher values partly because AMCs value the metal at slightly different times. Not investment advice.
