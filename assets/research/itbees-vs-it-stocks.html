Diagnosis

The opposite result to the pharma study. Here the wrapper, not the sector, is the problem.

The ETF lost to its own median stock — and to the plain Nifty.

Over its actual life ITBEES returned 15.2% a year against a median constituent's 21.2%. 7 of its 10 holdings beat it, and 13 of the 16 IT names on this page did. It took a −42.1% worst drawdown to get there.

It also lost to the market on every axis at once: NIFTYBEES returned 16.6% with 13.1% volatility and a −16.1% drawdown, against the ETF's 21.5% and −42.1%. Higher return, two-thirds less pain. On Sharpe the fund ranks 14th of 19.

Those three tiles are the entire explanation. An equal-weighted basket of the five large caps returned 15.4% — within a rounding error of the ETF's 15.2%. The five mid caps returned 36.0%. The fund is 78.8% of the first group and 21.2% of the second, so it delivered almost exactly the large-cap outcome. Strip out just Infosys and TCS and the remaining eight, equal-weighted, returned 30.0% a year.

Fees and drift add a little more. Holding the fund's own current portfolio at fixed proportions and rebalancing would have returned 18.6% versus the ETF's 15.2%: cap-weighting mechanically carried more money in the leaders of the previous cycle, precisely the names that then de-rated. Part of that 3.4-point gap is the rebalancing bonus any fixed-weight scheme earns from volatile constituents, so do not bank all of it — but the direction is not in doubt.

Persistence — can you pick?

Almost no signal at all — with one very loud exception.

Rank correlation between consecutive periods averages +0.04, against +0.14 for pharma. It is essentially noise, and it flips sign repeatedly: +0.93 from 2020 into 2021, then −0.58 into 2022, +0.59 into 2024, −0.48 into 2025. The leaderboard changed hands almost every year — Persistent, KPIT, Zensar, Oracle Financial, LTIMindtree. Chasing last year's winner was a treadmill.

Persistent Systems beat the fund in all seven periods — 7/7, 60.7% a year, a Sharpe of 1.49, and the only IT name on this page with a shallower drawdown than the ETF at −36.0%. ₹1 lakh became ₹18.1 lakh. Unlike Torrent in the pharma study, it is not impossibly priced either: a P/E of 43.4 and P/B of 11.0 against 29% sales and 36% profit growth over five years, on a 27.2% ROE.

The other side — what has actually changed

This is where the IT case inverts the pharma case. The record is worse; the entry point is far better.

You would be buying near a five-year relative low

The pharma fund was at 100% of its 52-week range and the 100th percentile of its five-year ratio to the Nifty. ITBEES is the mirror image: 29.3% below its December 2024 peak, at 43% of its 52-week range, and its ratio to NIFTYBEES sits at the 7th percentile of the whole window — 41.2% below its own peak relative level, reached in December 2021.

Valuations follow. Nifty IT trades at a P/E of 18.05 with a 3.53% dividend yield — and the two names that caused the damage are the cheapest things in it: Infosys at 15.3× with a 4.08% yield, TCS at 16.5× with a 2.61% yield on a 51.8% ROE. The drag stocks have already de-rated. The sector has also bounced 22.2% off its 1 July 2026 low.

But the de-rating has a reason, and it is not cyclical

IT is India's worst sector of 2026 because the market is pricing AI as a threat to the billing model, not a tailwind. Nine major brokerages have flagged the same combination of AI-driven pricing deflation, weak discretionary spend and tariff pressure; sell-side estimates of AI-related revenue loss run to 9–12% of IT services revenue over three to four years. Industry revenue is still growing — NASSCOM puts FY26 at +6.1% to $315 bn — but constant-currency growth at the top firms has slowed to roughly 2.8%.

A P/E of 18 is only cheap if earnings hold. This is a genuine structural question, not a washed-out cycle, and it falls hardest on exactly the commoditised large-cap headcount businesses that make up 79% of this fund.

The diversification argument does not survive the test

ITBEES correlates 0.56 with NIFTYBEES — far above pharma's 0.26, because IT is itself a large slice of the Nifty. Blending 12% ITBEES into a Nifty-50 core over this window moved the CAGR from 16.62% to 16.75% and deepened the drawdown from −16.1% to −17.5%. Sharpe went 0.77 to 0.78. That is a rounding error, not a diversifier.

The risk profile is also worse than the headline suggests. ITBEES was negative over 41% of all rolling twelve-month windows, against 9% for NIFTYBEES, and its worst twelve months was −32.5% against the market's −5.5%. On rolling three-year returns it beat NIFTYBEES in just 8% of windows — and the equal-weight twin beat it in 100%.

Conclusion

What the evidence supports, what it does not, and what would change it.

On its own six-year record, ITBEES does not earn a place — and the reason is the wrapper, not the sector. The fund returned 15.2% a year while the median stock it holds returned 21.2% and an equal-weighted basket of the identical ten names returned 25.7%. It was beaten by 7 of its 10 holdings, by 13 of the 16 IT names on this page, and by the plain Nifty on every measure that matters at once: 16.6% versus 15.2% on return, 13.1% versus 21.5% on volatility, −16.1% versus −42.1% on drawdown, 0.77 versus 0.40 on Sharpe. ₹1 lakh became ₹2.37 lakh in the ETF, ₹2.56 lakh in NIFTYBEES, and ₹4.03 lakh in the equal-weight twin. This is the mirror image of the pharma result, where the ETF beat its median constituent at half the drawdown.

The mechanism is arithmetic, not misfortune. Free-float cap-weighting put 50.8% of the fund into Infosys and TCS, which compounded at 10.8% and 5.4%, and 5.7% into Persistent, which compounded at 60.7%. The five large caps equal-weighted returned 15.4% — indistinguishable from the ETF's 15.2% — while the five mid caps returned 36.0%. Since the fund is 78.8% large-cap by construction, it delivered the large-cap outcome almost exactly. It did not pick badly; it sized badly, and the sizing rule is the index, so it will keep doing so whenever the largest free floats are the slowest compounders. That is the same cap-weighting drag the ten-year pharma proxy showed, except here it happened inside the live window.

The honest case for buying is the entry point, and it is a real one. Everything above is backward-looking, and the setup today is the opposite of pharma's. ITBEES sits 29.3% below its December 2024 peak, at the 7th percentile of its ratio to the Nifty and 41.2% below its own peak relative level. Nifty IT trades at 18.05× earnings with a 3.53% yield, and the two stocks that did the damage are now the cheapest in it — Infosys at 15.3× on a 4.08% yield, TCS at 16.5× on a 51.8% ROE. Cap-weighting is a drag when your biggest holdings are expensive; it is a smaller drag when they are the cheapest things in the basket. If large-cap IT mean-reverts, this fund is the most direct way to own that, and it has already bounced 22.2% off the 1 July low.

What the study cannot settle is whether the de-rating is a cycle or a repricing. A P/E of 18 is cheap only if earnings hold. The market is not pricing a soft patch — it is pricing AI as a threat to per-seat billing, with sell-side estimates of 9–12% revenue loss over three to four years, against industry revenue still growing 6.1%. That question falls hardest on commoditised large-cap headcount businesses, which are 79% of this fund. The one thing this window says clearly is that if you are wrong about it, the cap-weighted vehicle is where you will feel it most.

So: if you want Indian IT, do not buy it cap-weighted. Prefer an equal-weight or mid-cap-tilted route — 25.7% and 36.0% respectively, both with shallower drawdowns than the ETF's −42.1%. If no such vehicle is available to you, the defensible fallback is to hold NIFTYBEES, which already carries roughly 10% IT and beat the sector fund outright, and to express any IT conviction through a capped satellite — Persistent is the only name here the evidence supports, at 7/7 periods and a P/E of 43.4 against 36% profit growth, sized at no more than 3–4%. If you buy ITBEES anyway, buy it as a valuation trade on large-cap IT rather than as a diversifier, size it small, stage the entry, and know that its 0.56 correlation to the Nifty means it will do almost nothing for portfolio risk.

What would change this. Toward the fund: NSE loosening the cap-weight rule, or large-cap IT earnings holding through FY27 while the multiple re-rates from 18×. Toward equal-weight: nothing — the case is already made in 100% of rolling three-year windows. Away from IT entirely: constant-currency growth at the top five turning negative, or AI-linked pricing deflation showing up in reported realisations rather than only in commentary. Review at 90 days against the Nifty IT TRI, not the price index.

The one-line version: the pharma ETF was a shallower hole than its own stocks, so it earned its place; the IT ETF is a shallower hole that also gave up six points a year to its own median holding and lost to the plain Nifty outright — the sector may well be cheap, but this is the wrong instrument for it.
