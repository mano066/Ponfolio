The honest caveats

Read these before acting on any number above

  • Fourteen years is one regime, not a law. Every blend I tested did better in 2020–26 than in 2013–19. That is not proof small and mid caps are robust — it means the whole period favoured them. A 1994–2003 style decade would invert this entire table.
  • These are category medians, and you cannot buy a median. Every figure assumes you held the middle fund of the category. Pick a bottom-quartile fund and you lose several points a year to the numbers shown.
  • Survivorship bias inflates all of it. Only funds still reporting NAV today are in the data. Schemes that died along the way are invisible, so every return here is flattered.
  • The "top 3" benchmark is unbuyable by construction — it is chosen after the year has finished. No fixed blend beats it on average; the best possible falls short. Treat it as a ceiling, not a target.
  • Small-cap returns come with real pain. The max-return blend lost 14.5% in 2018 and finished last of all categories in both 2018 and 2025. A calendar year understates it — intra-year drawdowns were far deeper.
  • Not advice. Educational analysis of public NAV data, and past calendar years carry no promise about the next one.

Method. 841 Direct-plan Growth funds with usable month-end NAV history, grouped into 19 SEBI categories having complete 2013–2026 coverage. A category's monthly index is the median month-on-month NAV move of its funds; a category-year return is the median fund's calendar-year return. A blend's yearly return is the contribution-weighted average of its sleeves; its corpus is the exact sum of independent monthly SIPs, since a never-rebalanced split makes each sleeve independent. All 1,832,616 blends of three to five categories in 1,000 steps were enumerated. Nifty 50 calendar-year price returns from Yahoo Finance (^NSEI). Data through 7 August 2026; 2026 is a part year.
